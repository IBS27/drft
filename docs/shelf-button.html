The shelf's new-thought button

The current affordance — ● CATCH A THOUGHT as bare text — fails twice. The copy is twee, and the form has no figure-ground: on a screen where every action is already quiet tracked caps, one more quiet tracked-caps line cannot read as the action. The primary act of the whole product deserves a shape.

On the label: NEW THOUGHT over COMPOSE. "Compose" is borrowed from email and treats the fragment as a document; "new thought" names the object the product is actually made of, stays inside the vocabulary rules, and pairs naturally with the dot the same way ● KEEP does on capture. All variants below use it; swapping the string is trivial if you prefer "compose."

01

Hairline capsule

The quietest thing that still reads as a button: the existing label, enclosed. Stillness says "hairlines instead of boxes" — this uses the one sanctioned line-weight to draw the one enclosure on the screen. Because nothing else on the shelf has a border, the capsule owns the eye despite being one pixel deep.

03

Ink pill — recommended

Invert the two tones the system already owns: ink fill, page-colored label, the vermilion dot burning inside. This is unambiguous — the darkest (light mode) or brightest (dark mode) object on the screen, unmistakably pressable, and it spends zero new color. It gives the shelf what capture's ● KEEP never needed, because keep sits alone on an empty screen and this button competes with a list. Strong figure over quiet ground is the most Stillness-native way to say "primary action."

04

The dot, grown

No words at all: the vermilion dot scaled to a 52pt circle, floating above the safe area. The dot already means "now" everywhere it appears — on keep, on waiting rows — so the biggest dot in the product marking "start now" is coherent, and it is the most confident, most ownable mark of the five.

Vermilion pill

07

Vermilion, reshaped

Same color commitment as 06, four other bodies for it. These vary along two axes: how much surface the color claims, and where the button lives — floating center, full bleed, corner, or fused to the edge the capture sheet actually rises from.

07a · Outlined capsule. The color as a line, not a mass: 1.5px vermilion stroke, vermilion label, transparent fill. The most Stillness-literate way to use the accent on a button — a hairline that burns. Reads clearly as pressable in light mode; in dark mode the orange stroke glows nicely against #131311.

What the full frame reveals: the pill leaves the page's air intact — the color floats in whitespace and the list stays the loudest thing. The bar closes the screen with a colored floor; it anchors harder and thumbs cannot miss it, but every glance at the shelf now ends on the button rather than on your own words. Worth judging on exactly that: which thing should the eye leave the screen remembering?

Recommendation: 03, the ink pill, labeled NEW THOUGHT. It is the only variant that fully answers "make it look like a button" without new colors, new shadows, or respending the vermilion budget — and it inherits the draft-preview behavior cleanly. Runner-up: 01, if the pill proves too loud on-device — or 06, if it proves not loud enough. All three share geometry, so trying them in the simulator is a one-line change each. Pressed state in all variants: opacity to ~70%, no scale, no bounce — Stillness doesn't jump.

Decision (on-device): 07b, the full-width vermilion bar — it won the thumb test despite the recommendation above. The spec (apps/ios/SPEC.md) and ShelfView record it as the shipped treatment.
