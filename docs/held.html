Held thoughts

Some thoughts you want close for a while — the ones you're circling this week, the ones you want to read again together. This adds one mark you make yourself and one page that gathers every marked thought. Web only; the phone's shelf and the daily email don't change.

The mark is a single on/off switch, like rest — not a tag, a folder or a second list you maintain. It has no due date, no count that grows into guilt, and no "done". Taking it off is one click. Part 01 is the name, part 02 is how you make the mark, and part 03 is the page, which is the main choice.

01

What to call it

"Review" is the word you used, but drft's vocabulary rules rule out words that make a thought something to get through ("done", "inbox", "process"), and "review" reads that way: a queue to clear. The name decides how the page feels, so it's worth choosing on purpose. The mocks below use Hold / Held; any name here works in any direction.

NameThe action · the pageWhy / why not
Hold · Held"Hold" on a thought · a "Held" pagePairs with rest: you let a thought rest, or you hold it close. Short, physical, no deadline in it. Chosen.
Keep close"Keep close" · "Close at hand"Warmest wording, but two words for a button, and "keep" is already the capture verb.
Sit with"Sit with this" · "Sitting with"Uses the product's own language ("sat with"), but it's awkward as a page name in the rail.
Pin · Pinned"Pin" · "Pinned"Everyone knows it, but "pinned" suggests stuck to the top of the collection, which this isn't.
Review"Mark for review" · "Review"Clearest about the purpose, but it brings task-manager weight into a room built to avoid it.
02

Making the mark

Wherever the mark is made, it should look the same everywhere: a neutral bookmark in ink, never vermilion. Vermilion stays the colour of now (today's returned thought, the open row). A held thought is for later, so it gets the quieter ink. Pressing h toggles it from the thought view and from a highlighted rail row.

A Chosen

A chip beside the thought

The thought view already gathers small chips under your words: Returned today, Set down. Holding adds one more. Before it's held, it's a dashed "Hold" chip that only shows when you hover over the thought (and always on touch). Once held, it's a solid chip that says when: Held · 3 days. Click it again to let go. The single action at the bottom of the page stays Let it rest, as the experience doc requires.

C Optional, with A

From the rail, without opening the thought

Hovering a row in the edge rail shows a small bookmark button at the end of the row, in place of its age. Held rows then carry a small before the age, so you can tell them apart in the collection without an extra section. This works alongside A or B rather than replacing them; on its own it's hard to find.

03

The page — five directions

Every direction is reached the same way: a Held entry in the rail, under Find, with a quiet count (shown only when something is held). Below the rail's breakpoint it's a bookmark icon in the header beside search and settings. The directions differ in how much of each thought the page shows and how you move through them.

01

The list

The same shape as the Resting page: one row per thought, the first line verbatim, how long it's been held, and the first line of your notes under it when there are any. Clicking a row opens the thought; j/k step through only the held thoughts. The quietest option and the closest to what's already built.

02

Read them through

One continuous column you read top to bottom. Each held thought is shown in full and close to its thought-view size, with the start of your notes on their ruled lines and the thoughts it's related to as chips. A related chip that is also held is tinted, so you can see where your held ideas touch each other. Hovering an entry shows Open and Let go. This is the page that does the reviewing: you can read everything you're holding in one sitting, without clicking.

03 Chosen

Lay them out

Every held thought as a card, spread across the page like index cards on a table. Because you see them all at once, you compare them instead of reading them one by one, which is the point of holding them. The rail stays on the left as on every other page, so Held is a room in the same building, not a separate app.

Three columns beside the rail at most laptop and desktop widths, four from about 1800px, two when the window is narrow.

The card

Your words, in full. Never cut to one line the way the rail does. A very long thought stops at about ten lines and fades, and the full text is one click away.
Your notes, a short excerpt. The first three lines of the thought's notes, behind the same vermilion margin rule as the notebook lines in the thought view, so it's clearly your writing about the thought.
How long it's been held. "held 3d", in the same small grey as every age in the app. No warning colours as it gets older.
2 — links on this table. Only counts related thoughts that are also held, because those are the ones you can see here. Related thoughts that aren't held stay on the thought page.

The card has no buttons until you hover it. Clicking the card opens the thought; the rail then marks it as open, and Esc brings you back to the table where you left off.

Seeing which ones are related

This is what a table can show and a list can't. Hover a card (or move focus to it with the keyboard) and the cards it's related to get an outline and a small Related label, while the rest fade back. Two held thoughts you never thought of together turn out to be next to each other. The hovered card shows one button in its corner, the filled bookmark, to let go of the hold; h does the same. The card closes up, and an undo strip appears for a few seconds, the same as when you set a related thought aside.

Why two orders

In the first draft I gave the page a When held / When kept switch, because there are two honest ways to order the cards:

OrderWhat it showsVerdict
When heldNewest hold first: what you're thinking about this week comes first. Holds you've kept for a long time drift to the end, a quiet hint that they might be ready to let go.Keep, as the default. Renamed Recent.
When keptBy when you first wrote the thought down, so a thought from June can sit beside its echo from October. It shows how your thinking moved over time.Drop it. The cards' dates already give you this, and the order is hard to read on a table: card order across uneven columns isn't obvious. Its real goal, putting echoes side by side, is done better by the next option.
TogetherRelated held thoughts sit next to each other in rows. Each row is a group that's linked, strongest links first, and the thoughts that aren't near any others share the last row.Replace "When kept" with this. It uses the space for what space is good at: things that belong together, together.

So the switch becomes Recent · Together. The groups have no names or titles: a name would have to be written by the AI, and drft doesn't put generated words on the page. You can tell what joins a group by reading it.

About the "no grid of cards" rule

The experience doc says the collection shouldn't be a grid of cards. The reason is that cards turn every thought into an item to manage, and the collection is everything you've ever captured. Held is a different case: a small set you picked by hand, for the purpose of looking at them together. The cards stay quiet — no colours per card, no drag-to-arrange, no pinning to positions — so the page is a table you read, not a board you manage. The collection itself keeps its list.

Shows the whole held set at once, and the hover highlight shows which ones are related.
Together groups echoes side by side without anyone having to tag or sort.
It's the one place that uses cards, so it has to stay quiet to fit with the rest. Beyond about 30 held thoughts the page gets long, which is itself a hint to let some go.
04

One at a time

A focused sitting. One held thought fills the page, with your notes beneath it, editable right there. ←/→ move to the next one. A row of dots shows where you are in the set, without numbers. Let go drops the hold and moves on. Good for going deep on each one, like the thought view on repeat.

05

How they connect

Held thoughts as dots, with the related thoughts that join them drawn as lines, beside a short list. The connections graph already exists in the app; this is that graph narrowed to what you're holding. Hovering a list row lights up its dot and its links. Held thoughts that aren't directly related can still join through a thought you're not holding (dashed lines, small open dots), which is often the interesting part.

04

Behaviour, whichever you pick

CaseProposed
OrderMost recently held first. Holding is a "now" gesture; the oldest holds sink to the bottom, where it's obvious they've been there a while, without a nag saying so.
Resting a held thoughtResting also lets go of the hold: a thought that's been set down shouldn't still be waiting for you. Waking it doesn't bring the hold back.
Daily emailUnchanged. Selection never ranks thoughts, so holding doesn't make one come back sooner. The page is where you go to them yourself.
Nothing heldThe rail entry goes away and the page shows one line: "Hold a thought to keep it here." No empty state illustration, no hint of something to do.
LimitNone. If it grows long, that's useful to see, and the oldest holds are the obvious ones to let go.
iOSNot part of this. The shelf ignores the mark. It's a new optional field, so the app keeps working as-is.
DataOne optional heldAt timestamp on the thought, plus an index by user and heldAt so the page reads newest-held first, the same way the resting list uses restedAt.

Decided

  1. Name — Hold / Held.
  2. Mark — A, the chip beside the thought, plus h. C (from the rail) still open.
  3. Page — 03, Lay them out, with a Recent · Together switch.
