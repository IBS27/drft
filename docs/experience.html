The Experience

drft

What exactly happens, moment by moment — from the second a thought occurs to the day you set it down.

01Three Moments

The whole product is three moments, each with its own device, pace, and rules. Everything else — schema, AI, delivery — exists to serve one of these.

Seconds · iPhone

Capture

A thought occurs at the worst possible time. You get it out of your head and back to your life in under ten seconds.

One breath · Email

Return

Once a day, one old thought finds you. Reading it is the whole interaction — the subject line is your own words, and it asks nothing.

Minutes · Web

Develop

When you choose to sit down, the collection is waiting — a thought, and the older ones it turned out to echo. You read, you follow a link, you stop when you feel like stopping.

The division of labor: the phone plants seeds, the daily email waters them, the web is where you sit with them. No moment ever demands the next one. drft never says "you have 12 unprocessed thoughts."

02Capture — iOS, capture first

You're mid-walk and think: cities feel more creative because collisions are free. You tap the lock-screen widget.

What you see

  • One screen: an empty text field with the keyboard already up, a speak button, a keep button. Nothing else.
  • You type or dictate the fragment exactly as it came — no title, no tags, no folder, no "where should this go?"
  • Tap keep. A single vermilion dot acknowledges it, the screen dismisses itself. Done.

What you don't see

The mutation lands the raw text instantly, then an async action embeds it, searches for resonant older thoughts, and links the ones that hold up. All of it stays silent. The links are made, but nothing announces them — nothing notifies, nothing replies, capture never becomes the start of a conversation you didn't ask for.

The phone captures and glances; development stays seated. Capture is the app — launch always lands on the empty field, keyboard up. Behind it sits the shelf: a quiet, non-editing view of the collection you can pull down into, re-read, and set a thought to rest from. What the phone never gets is the desk — no wake, no search, no tending the links. Re-exposure belongs anywhere; development is a sitting-down activity, and the web is where you sit. The original phase-4 rule ("no list, no reading on the phone") was half shipping pragmatism, half rhythm protection; the shelf revises the first half and keeps the second — the phone must never become an obligation surface, so the shelf shows no counts, sends no notifications, and asks for nothing.

Rules of capture

  • Capture must never fail visibly. If the network is down, the fragment is queued on-device and the dot still confirms. (Queue implementation can come later; the promise is day one.)
  • Capture must never ask a question. Any decision — even one tap of choice — is friction that loses the fleeting ones.
  • Verbatim in. The AI never rewrites, titles, or "cleans up" a fragment. Your words are the record.

03Return — one thought a day, by email

At a time you chose once in settings — say 8:30, with coffee — one email arrives. The subject line is your own words, verbatim:

drft · 8:30 · subject

recipes as version control — forks & merges

body

You kept this two weeks ago.

Reading it is the whole interaction. The subject line does the work on its own — the thought is back in your head from the inbox list, before you open anything. The body carries one plain line of provenance and one quiet link into the workspace, for the evenings when it itches. It replants the idea and asks for nothing. Maybe it dissolves into your morning. Maybe you open drft that night. Both are success; the loop's job is re-exposure, not clicks.

How the day's thought is chosen

  • Never the newest. Fresh thoughts don't need help returning; the loop exists for week-old and month-old ones.
  • A slow rotation, not a score. Never-resurfaced thoughts come first; after that, whichever eligible thought has waited longest returns. Nothing ranks a thought's promise or its links — every fragment eventually gets its turn.
  • One per day, maximum. Scarcity is what makes it a ritual instead of noise. If nothing has genuinely accumulated (early days, thin collection), drft stays silent — silence beats filler.
  • Resting thoughts are exempt. Once you set a thought down, it is never sent to you again.

Why email, and not a push notification

  • The medium already behaves the way the product wants to. Email is read-and-let-lie by default; nothing about it demands a reply. And the inbox row is a lock screen you already check — the re-exposure lands whether or not you open the message.
  • A push would make the return a demand. Even now that the phone's shelf gives a tap target something to land on, a notification turns the return moment into an interruption that expects handling — the opposite of read-and-let-lie. The shelf pins today's returned thought for whoever wanders in; email remains the channel that goes out.
  • It arrives on time, everywhere. A Convex cron sends at the chosen minute, and it reaches the phone through Mail, so the morning-with-coffee moment survives intact. Web push would only fire if a browser happened to be awake, and would land at the desk — pressure to click, at the wrong hour.
  • Nothing to install, nothing to renew. No device tokens, no APNs keys, no dependency on the iOS app being installed and re-signed. (Push would also require a paid Apple Developer account: free personal-team provisioning grants App Groups, but never aps-environment.)

The risk email carries, and the rules that answer it. An inbox is an obligation surface — one unread drft is fine, seven is guilt, which is precisely what "drft never says you have 12 unprocessed thoughts" exists to prevent. So: one a day, hard maximum. Never a follow-up, never a digest, never a "you haven't opened these." An unread daily thought is not a debt; it already did its job in the subject line, and it is never mentioned again.

Selection is the phase; delivery is an adapter. What ships in phase 5 is the scheduler that decides which thought comes back today — channel-agnostic logic, and where all the product risk lives. Email is the first adapter behind it. If the daily thought proves worth receiving, APNs can be added later as a second adapter without touching selection.

Why this can't be an afterthought: the success metric is "thoughts came back to me and became clearer." This daily email is the only mechanism that runs without the user doing anything. It's the product's heartbeat — which is why it ships last (phase 5), once weeks of real captures exist to tune it against.

04Develop — the web workspace

That evening you open drft on your laptop. This is the only surface with any density, and it's still one room:

The collection

  • Thoughts listed by time — today / this week / earlier — first line verbatim, nothing more. No grid of cards, no previews, no counts nagging you.
  • If today's resurfaced thought exists, it sits at the top, held slightly apart, carrying the vermilion dot. The dot marks now: it is the only color in the room and the only suggestion of where to look.
  • ⌘K searches, for the evening you're after one thought in particular; j/k step through the collection and Esc comes back to it. That is the entire chrome.

The thought view

You click the recipes fragment. Your words sit large in the middle of an otherwise empty page, exactly as captured. Everything else is a quiet line of margin around them:

  • Related thoughts as small chips (edited silence · attention is a garden) — auto-linked by resonance while you were away, one click to jump, one click to set a bad link aside. This is the whole navigation model: thoughts lead to thoughts.
  • A returned · today line if the daily email brought it back, so the page and the inbox agree about why you're here.
  • If the thought is resting: set down · three weeks ago, and the single line you left about where it landed.
  • One action at the foot of the page — let it rest, or wake if it's already resting. Nothing else asks anything of you.
The page never speaks. Nothing is generated into it, nothing is written on your behalf, nothing waits for a reply. The AI's entire visible output is a handful of links between your own fragments — and each of those you can dismiss. Re-reading your own words, with the two or three others they turn out to touch, is the development.

Setting a thought down

Some thoughts, after enough sittings, feel finished — not "completed," just done being thought about. One quiet action: let it rest, with an optional single line about where it landed ("became the pitch for the recipes app", or nothing at all). A resting thought leaves the collection and the daily rotation, but stays searchable and linkable — it can still surface under a newer fragment as set down · recipes as version control. Rest is reversible with one click; some thoughts wake up.

05A Lifecycle Without Stages

A thought's real arc — first thought → revisit → sit with it (often for a long time) → set it down — is something the user does, not a status the system displays. There is no pipeline, no maturity meter, no progress bar. A thought that's been circled for six months isn't "stuck"; it's being thought about. That's the product working.

What exists in the systemWhat the user experiences
open The default and only state a thought is ever "in." It appears in the collection, accrues links to what it echoes, and is eligible for the daily return. How developed it is shows only through its own accumulation — the length of its margin, never a badge.
resting The one transition, and the user makes it: let it rest, optional closing line. Out of the collection and the rotation; still searchable, still linkable, still part of your thinking's history. Reversible.
Vocabulary is load-bearing. Never "done," "complete," "archive," "inbox," or "process." Thoughts are kept, revisited, sat with, set down. The moment the interface implies a thought is supposed to go somewhere, drft has become the task manager it exists to not be.

06What drft Refuses to Do

Anti-features, stated so they stay refused:

  • No folders, tags, or manual organization — resonance links are the only structure.
  • No tasks, reminders, or due dates — the moment a thought has a deadline it belongs in another tool.
  • No streaks, counts-as-guilt, or gamification — the collection count is a fact, never a scoreboard.
  • No feed, no social, no sharing (v1) — this is a private room.
  • No AI-generated content masquerading as yours — every word in a fragment is the user's.
  • No chat, no assistant voice, no generated prose — the AI works in the background and its only visible output is a link between two of your own thoughts, which you can set aside.
  • No more than one message a day, ever — and no follow-up about an unread one.

07What This Pins Down for the Foundation

Phase 1 can now be built against decisions instead of guesses:

TableShape it must have
thoughts verbatim text, createdAt, status: "open" | "resting", optional restingNote + restedAt, embedding (vector index). No title, no tags, no category — their absence is the product.
connections fromId, toId, score, dismissedAt? — auto-linked from the embedding, user-dismissable, never user-curated. The only structure in the product.
resurfacings log of userId + thoughtId + date — powers "one per day, never repeat too soon, never resting thoughts." Phase 5 adds channel + deliveredAt?; the email adapter also freezes its exact emailPayload? before sending so retries are idempotent. Selection remains channel-agnostic. (Table lands in phase 1; the scheduler that writes it is phase 5.)
  • Capture mutation stays dumb: insert text + timestamp, schedule enrichment, return. Everything intelligent is async.
  • The web capture input for dogfooding (phase 1) obeys the same capture rules as iOS: one field, keep, no questions asked.
  • Settings are nearly empty by design: the daily email time is the only real preference the product has — and because the server sends it, that preference lives in Convex, not on a device.
